# THINK: HarmonyOS device-federation mechanisms — for the OT-42 sovereign super-device direction

**Date:** 2026-07-03
**Source:** deep-research pass (96 agents; 21/23 claims verified, 2 refuted). Primaries: the **OpenHarmony/openEuler `communication_dsoftbus` + `security_deviceauth` repos** (open-source proxy for the commercial Super Device), the **peer-reviewed IEEE study "Breaking the Trust Circle in HarmonyOS"** (DarkNavy), and Promon + Huawei dev docs. **Status:** research note feeding **OT-42** (the sovereign personal super-device Phase-N direction). Not a build.

## 0. Headline
The research gives us a **concrete federation architecture to learn from** AND the **single load-bearing security lesson** for OT-42. HarmonyOS builds a device federation on **DSoftBus** (a userspace distributed bus, LAN/near-field only) with cross-device trust rooted in **DeviceAuth/HiChain**, and — crucially — it supports **account-irrelevant (cloud-free) peer-to-peer trust groups**: devices form a trust ring from a **shared secret/PIN → session key → encrypted channel**, with **no central operator**. That P2P, no-cloud grouping is the most directly transferable primitive for a *sovereign single-owner* federation. **But** HarmonyOS's substrate is **identity/permission-based, not object-capability**, its trust is **transitive**, and app integrity is anchored to **Huawei's root certificate** — all three conflict with our commitments, and the transitive-trust choice is *exactly* the one a peer-reviewed exploit broke.

## 1. What to adopt (the mechanism template)
- **Account-irrelevant P2P trust ring (adopt the model).** The owner's own devices form a trust group from a shared secret, no cloud account. Satisfies owner-sovereignty directly. *(Verified 3-0 against `security_deviceauth`.)*
- **The DeviceAuth bootstrap pattern (adopt, reimplement).** shared-secret/PIN → mutual auth → session key → per-session encrypted channel; full lifecycle (establish/maintain/use/cancel); a small API surface (create-group / add-member / auth-device / process-data). **NeoPlutarch's version:** replace HiChain's crypto with a **formally-verifiable Rust attestation**, and — the key change — **bind the session key to a capability, not to a device identity.**
- **The LAN/near-field locality boundary (adopt — it's a security asset).** DSoftBus only connects same-LAN / near-field devices; no WAN exposure. Keep that boundary; the federation is physically scoped.
- **Bind-before-connect (adopt).** Trust binding/attestation must precede any cross-device connection.

## 2. What to reject (where HarmonyOS conflicts with our commitments)
- **Transitive trust — REJECT (this is the big one).** HarmonyOS trust is transitive (A↔B, B↔C ⇒ A trusts C) and the bus delegates its security entirely to the trust-circle auth. A peer-reviewed IEEE study **chained four 0-days to bypass the "same account" check and inject a rogue device** — and because trust is transitive, **one compromised device breaks the whole federation.** NeoPlutarch must prefer **explicit, per-capability, revocable, NON-transitive cross-device delegation** over an all-or-nothing "same-account" ring.
- **Identity/ambient permissions — REJECT.** HarmonyOS gates cross-device RPC by *named permissions* (`ohos.permission.DISTRIBUTED_DATASYNC/…SOFTBUS_CENTER`) checked at the IPC boundary, mapping app *identity* (Access Token Manager / TokenID) to stored grants — ambient authority, not unforgeable delegatable capabilities. Keep our capability-indirection substrate.
- **Central root of trust — REJECT.** App integrity chains to **Huawei's root certificate** — a hard central-operator dependency. In a sovereign OS **the owner is their own root of trust**; attestation anchors to an owner-held key, not a vendor CA.
- **The bus is TCB — MIND IT.** DSoftBus's session/discovery layer becomes part of the TCB for any cross-device capability, and DeviceAuth is a *swappable userspace module* there. For us the cross-device attestation + delegation path is security-critical and must be inside the audited/verified surface, not a pluggable peripheral.

## 3. The answer to OT-42's load-bearing question
OT-42 flagged the design question a future ratification must resolve: *"how does the capability graph delegate/revoke across the owner's device ring without a central operator, and how does I-14 hold across the bus?"* This research sharpens the answer into a **design stance**:
- **Non-transitive, per-capability, attestation-gated, revocable delegation.** A specific capability is delegated to a specific attested device for a bounded scope — not "admit a whole device to a trust group and rely on ambient permission checks." (The research found HarmonyOS only does the latter; the per-capability cross-device *token* was **not** found — see §4 — so this is ours to design.)
- **I-14 across the federation:** the network + every peer device is new attack surface; transitive trust propagates compromise. So I-14 fault-containment must extend across the bus, and the delegation model must ensure **a compromised peer device cannot exceed the specific capabilities delegated to it** (the containment analog of "a driver fault can't mutate governance").
- **Owner as root of trust; LAN-scoped; the attestation path in the verified surface.**

## 4. Honest gaps (what two passes did NOT establish)
- **The Harmony *intent framework* (the Arthur analog) is now 0-for-2.** Both research passes produced **zero verified claims** on Harmony Intents / Intent-as-a-Service / Agent Framework 2.0 / Celia and its NL→intent→permission-check→action model. Every public source found was rated *unreliable* (dev.to/medium reposts). **Interpretation:** the intent-framework permission model is either thinly documented or proprietary; a third generic web pass is unlikely to help. **Recommendation:** design Arthur's intent/permission model from **our own principles** (we already are — A1/A2/A3 + the 29.3 cap-check + the yolo posture) rather than wait on HarmonyOS evidence. If we want their model specifically, it needs primary Huawei ArkTS Intents-Kit docs, not search.
- **Touch-to-Share authority transfer** and **Star Shield granular privacy** also produced no confirmed claims — only the general DSoftBus/DeviceAuth trust mechanisms were verified. Whether *authority* (not just data) transfers on a Touch-to-Share handoff is unresolved.
- **Source caveat:** findings rest on the open-source OpenHarmony repos (a fair but imperfect proxy for the commercial Super Device, which may add proprietary cloud-bridging *above* the LAN-only open bus). The IEEE vulns are patched — the takeaway is the *architectural lesson* (transitive trust), not a live exploit.

## 5. Net for OT-42
The federation architecture is now understood well enough to design ours *deliberately different*: **an owner-sovereign, LAN-scoped, capability-bound, non-transitive device-federation** — taking HarmonyOS's cloud-free P2P trust-ring bootstrap and locality boundary, while rejecting its transitive trust, ambient/identity permissions, and vendor root-of-trust. The **non-transitive per-capability delegation** stance is the concrete thing to carry into a future ratification. Still Phase-N; still gated; the current single-device N=1 commitment stands.
