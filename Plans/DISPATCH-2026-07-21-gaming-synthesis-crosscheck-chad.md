## THE ANCHOR QUESTION (the PL's central concern — lead with it)
**With all these isolation layers, is gaming actually FAST AND FLUID — or does "near-native" quietly leak frames and input latency?** A gamer feels a dropped frame and 1–2 ms of input lag. The synthesis promises near-native performance through a **bounded hypervisor → VT-d GPU passthrough → cap-broker seam → Gamescope→G1 present seam → brokered input/audio**. **Pressure-test that promise, layer by layer — this is the make-or-break claim:**
1. **GPU passthrough overhead.** VFIO/VT-d GPU passthrough is *industry-proven* near-native (people game on it daily) — but quantify honestly: IOMMU translation, MMIO trap cost, interrupt remapping latency; and does *our* bounded hypervisor add overhead beyond stock VFIO/KVM? Is the GPU truly talking to hardware directly, or is anything trapped per-submit?
2. **The cap-broker seam (the risk I'm most worried about).** Every broker (GPU-submit, input, audio, present) is a potential copy/latency/context-switch. Is the seam **zero-copy** for the frame path? Does GPU submit go *directly* to the passed-through device (near-native) or through a broker that traps/copies (deadly)? What's the realistic per-frame + per-input-event overhead the brokers add?
3. **The present seam — Gamescope→G1 (the sneaky one).** SteamOS works partly because **Gamescope *is* the display owner** — one compositor. Our synthesis hands frames from Gamescope to **G1** — does that add a **compositor hop = up to a frame of latency**, and does it **break VRR / tearing-control / frame-pacing**? Is there a zero-copy, VRR-preserving handoff, or should the game path *bypass* G1 in game-mode (Gamescope owns the display directly)? This may be the single biggest fluidity risk — dig in.
4. **Input & audio latency.** Controller → USB → broker → guest → game: does cap-brokering add measurable input lag? Low-latency audio through a broker — realistic?
5. **CPU/scheduling.** The guest game-loop under our hypervisor — CPU pinning, no vCPU steal, real-time-ish scheduling. Does our scheduler starve the game, or can it pin cleanly?
**Verdict on §1: honest realistic frame-rate + input-latency delta vs bare-metal Linux gaming — and WHERE the layers cost, if they do. If any layer breaks fluidity, say so loudly; that's the finding that matters most to the PL.**

## The load-bearing architecture claims (pressure-test each)
6. **BUILD-vs-DELEGATE → DELEGATE.** Is delegating the GPU via passthrough genuinely the right call vs a driver-in-kernel — and does it *really* keep the driver out of our TCB, or does the hypervisor pull enough GPU-adjacent code back in that the TCB win is smaller than claimed?
7. **The two-GPU requirement + single-iGPU-passthrough risk.** Is single-GPU passthrough (host loses its only GPU) as pivotal as claimed? Is two-GPU (discrete AMD guest) the honest requirement, or is single-GPU-with-a-fallback-console viable?
