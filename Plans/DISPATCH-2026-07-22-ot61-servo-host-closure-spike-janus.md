## The question (feasibility is the whole deliverable)
Chad's REFUTE killed the "small `no_std` strip of four crates" framing: Servo's useful layout/paint/DOM closure is a **hosted, threaded** engine, and SpiderMonkey is a C++ engine with its own host expectations. So: **what does that closure actually require from the host `std`/OS surface, what does our substrate provide, and how do we close the gap — bounded hosted services vs a maintained fork?**

   - **(A) A bounded hosted-Rust/POSIX-like "std-lite" personality** for the browser component ONLY — a narrow, capability-confined host-services layer (threads/TLS/sync/fs-shim/time) behind the OT-44 bundle boundary, so upstream Servo/SpiderMonkey compile against it without a general POSIX personality leaking into the ABI (the OT-61 ratified constraint: "no C++/POSIX personality at the ABI"). 4. **The `std`/unsupported-target mechanics.** Concretely: is the path a custom Rust target-spec (like our kernel target) with a `std` shim, a `no_std`+`alloc`+libneo-shim, or upstream's existing `unsupported`/`hermit`-style target work reused? 5. **The smallest de-risking prototype.** Name the one concrete "hello-closure" milestone that proves the surface is real (e.g. compile `stylo` — or an even smaller Servo crate — against the proposed target surface and run one unit of its logic), and whether it's buildable now or needs a precursor.
