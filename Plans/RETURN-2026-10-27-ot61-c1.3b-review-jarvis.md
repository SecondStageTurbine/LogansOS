## Checked failure schedules

**Finding 1 — refused kernel join retries forever before syscall return.**

1. P has W running on CPU 0 and J on CPU 1 (reachable after IPC migration). J enters SYS_THREAD_JOIN(W),
   before P is retiring, and claims W's not-done record. Stop J just before `futex::wait_on_key` in
   `kernel/src/sched/process.rs:2704`, with the done-word zero and no futex queue lock held.
2. W calls SYS_EXIT(7). `retire_process_from_current_thread` marks P retiring (`process.rs:2905`),
   purges futex entries (`:2918`) and finishes its ready/token scans (`:2924`). J is Running in
   the kernel, absent from all drained stores. W's process-exit path never sets its thread-join
   done-word; that publication belongs to `thread_exit_current`, not SYS_EXIT.
3. CPU 0 kicks CPU 1 (`:2938`). CPU 1's syscall has IF=0 from SFMASK (`syscall.rs:549` onward), so
   the IPI can remain pending. `sys_thread_join_impl` calls the join body directly (`:6900`), with
   no IF-enabling wrapper. If an IPI instead reaches a CPL0 point, its handler only EOIs.
4. J calls `wait_on_key`, which locks FUTEX_QUEUE and sees P.is_retiring, returning ESRCH
   (`kernel/src/sched/futex.rs:158`, `:163`). Its Process Arc and queue guard drop normally.
   But `process.rs:2704` discards that result with `let _ =`; the loop checks the still-zero
   done-word and retries immediately forever. J holds no persistent queue lock but stays in CPL0
   with IF=0. It never reaches the new syscall-return retire check at `syscall.rs:1244`.

Wrong result: CPU 1 spins in the kernel, P never reaches its final reap, and its parent's sys_wait
hangs. The new refusal fixes #99's late **raw futex** park, but turns the late **join** variant into
a kernel retry loop. This schedule works with wait-multiplex disabled and does not rely on a missing
timer on the AP: IF=0 also prevents a timer from rescuing it. Checking/propagating the terminal
ESRCH from the kernel join loop is required before the return-boundary mechanism can work.

**Finding 2 — an IPI can be consumed after the last check but before returning to ring 3.**

1. P has an exiting sibling on CPU 0 and thread T on AP CPU 1. T completes SYS_SPAWN and will spin
   in ring 3 without further syscalls after it returns. This is an actual IF=1-returning production
   path: `spawn_user` calls `shootdown_quiesce.resume()` at `process.rs:2283`, and
   `kernel/src/mm/tlb_shootdown.rs:39` through `:50` unconditionally enables interrupts at `:49`.
   `sys_spawn_impl` returns after its handle installation (`syscall.rs:1746` through `:1775`);
   intervening `without_interrupts` scopes restore the prior IF=1.
2. On CPU 1 the final `retire_current_if_dying` at `syscall.rs:1244` observes no retirement and
   returns. Interrupts remain enabled. Pause T in the dispatch epilogue or the assembly FPU/GP
   restore after `call syscall_dispatch` (`:755` or `:856`), before the later CLI (`:788`, `:891`).
   T is still CPL0 and owns no kernel lock at this point.
3. CPU 0 now marks P, drains all scheduler stores and sees CPU 1's correctly published pid P.
   It sends the IPI. CPU 1 takes it immediately in the IF=1 tail. `idt.rs:957` EOIs, and `:963`
   skips retirement because the interrupted CS was CPL0. No pending-retire latch or later check
   exists in this tail. This is the only retirement IPI, and it has been consumed.
4. CPU 1 resumes its tail, executes CLI/SWAPGS/IRETQ and runs T's ring-3 loop. No AP timer tick
   arrives in the reviewed default configuration and T makes no syscall, so T survives forever.

Wrong result: the process never completes, although targeting and delivery both succeeded. The
retirement check must cover the final transition through IRETQ, including syscalls returning IF=1;
the current late CLI protects GS handling but does not close this earlier last-check window.
This is a source-derived schedule, not a claimed injected-IPI hardware/QEMU measurement.

## Checklist

1. **FAIL — the ordering argument is insufficient to establish retirement (Finding 2).**
   Flag publication precedes purge/drain (`process.rs:2905`, `:2918`, `:2924`). All five scheduler
   current-thread replacements publish pid under the local scheduler lock (`scheduler.rs:899`,
   `:1828`, `:1885`, `:1947`, `:2019`); the final empty kill pass visits every initialized CPU's
   scheduler lock (`:2621` onward). This orders an already-running sibling's pid before targeting.
   Finding 2 still defeats the intended conclusion: the CPU is seen and kicked but cannot retire
   at its last boundary. Also the literal two-case proof has acknowledged exceptions: a BSP
   preemption can put the sibling Ready after the drain, and an in-flight IPC waker can hold its
   Box outside the drained stores. The next BSP timer or resumed IPC syscall return covers those
   ordinary cases; they require those third-case arguments, not a claim that every CPU was IPI'd.

2. **PASS — retire call sites themselves are safe boundary points.**
   `syscall.rs:947`, `:1244` call before/after dispatch locals; IRQ sites are CPL3-gated
   (`idt.rs:963`, `timer.rs:415`). The slow helper drops its temporary Arc before scheduler exit
   (`process.rs:3041`, `:3046`). No added check deliberately retires from CPL0 or inside a syscall
   body. Both timer asm variants push 15 registers, then one rbp anchor: RIP is at anchor+128,
   CS at +136. An executed source/frame calculation confirmed 16 pushes including the anchor in
   each variant, matching `timer_ring3_arg!` (`timer.rs:61`, `:167`, `:301`). Finding 2 concerns
   the unchecked interval after the return check, not the safety of calling it at its current site.

3. **PASS — IRQ lock and EOI discipline at the new call sites.**
   The reschedule IPI EOIs first (`idt.rs:957`); timer LAPIC/PIC EOI remains before expiry and
   retirement (`timer.rs:386`, `:389`, `:415`). Expiry/watchdog helpers return before the new check,
   retaining no guard. The interrupted CPL3 thread cannot own a kernel lock. `process::get`
   (`process.rs:1589`) and `scheduler::current_pid` (`scheduler.rs:1775`) mask interrupts around
   their locks; slow retirement drops the Arc before `scheduler::exit` takes its lock. A CPL0
   IPI does not take these new locks. Finding 1 is a retry-loop liveness failure, not an IRQ lock
   order failure or an EOI omission.

4. **FAIL — refusal is correct for raw futex waits but not every kernel caller (Finding 1).**
   `futex.rs:156` fetches the Arc before FUTEX_QUEUE; consuming `Option::is_some_and` at `:163`
   drops it before any park. The flag check and purge (`:469` onward) use the same queue lock, so
   a raw waiter either parks before purge or sees ESRCH; its dispatcher return can then retire it.
   std's futex wrapper returns true for non-timeout results (`std/neoplutarch_rt.rs:258`), which
   is safe when the syscall-return check runs before userland can retry. The kernel join loop does
   not return to that boundary on ESRCH. Therefore the checklist's blanket refused-waiter claim fails.

5. **FAIL — correct targeting can still lose the only useful interruption (Finding 2).**
   `per_cpu.rs:208`, `:216` publish/load with Release/Acquire, and `process.rs:3000` skips self
   and uninitialized schedulers. Stale-pid IPIs are harmless: the handler checks the current thread's
   own process. In-flight blocked wake resumes through a syscall boundary, as noted in item 1.
   But an IPI delivered in the final IF=1 CPL0 tail is consumed without a subsequent check.
   Pids have no recycling allocator (`NEXT_PID`, `process.rs:72`, `:376`); this statement assumes
   no u32 allocation-counter wrap, for which no exhaustion guard is present. No reuse is needed
   for either reported finding.

6. **PASS — counter pairing and leak behavior, with corrected ordering wording.**
   Exactly the first false->true swap increments (`process.rs:2905`, `:2908`); Process::drop
   decrements once if fault_killing is true (`:2989`). The local Arc spans the increment, and the
   initiating Thread retains its own Process anchor until reap, so no final drop can precede it.
   A leak leaves the slow-path filter on; it still checks only the caller's pid and own flag
   (`:3036`, `:3041`) and cannot retire an unrelated process. **The dispatch's literal claim that
   increment precedes flag visibility is false:** swap publishes the flag first. With another
   retirement already counted a peer can observe this new flag before its increment. With count
   zero an early boundary can skip, but the initiator still increments before drain and kick.
   This short gap does not establish a separate wrong-retire/underflow finding; both blocking
   schedules above occur with RETIRING already nonzero and do not depend on it.

7. **FAIL — fault kill inherits both incomplete guarantees.**
   `process.rs:2884` and `:2978` share `retire_process_from_current_thread`. Replacing W's SYS_EXIT
   in Finding 1 by W's ring-3 page fault still leaves its done-word zero; replacing CPU 0's exit
   in Finding 2 by that fault still sends the one consumed CPL0 IPI. The fault twin scenario is
   useful: `neo-worker/src/main.rs:667` faults with a CPU-2 user spinner and expects 139, so removing
   the kick leaves that spinner alive. It covers a preexisting ring-3 spinner, not these two seams.

8. **PASS — stated G1/G2/G3 roles discriminate their specific mechanisms; coverage is incomplete.**
   `neo-worker/src/main.rs:606` onward defines a no-syscall user spinner, raw late futex waiter
   and kernel spinner; `neo-init/src/main.rs:2554` onward checks role codes 7/139/7/7. The kernel
   late-park hook waits past retirement before queue entry (`futex.rs:242` onward), so absent
   refusal role 3 can park forever; absent kick role 1's AP spinner never stops. The 500ms kernel
   spin receives an IPI in CPL0, then reaches syscall return; without that check role 4 survives.
   Its hold restores entry IF=0 (`syscall.rs:6835`), so it cannot cover Finding 2's IF=1 return.
   Role 3 calls raw SYS_FUTEX_WAIT, never SYS_THREAD_JOIN, missing Finding 1. The disclosed BSP
   timer race is accurately not deterministically covered; next BSP tick covers that described
   case. It does not rescue the no-tick AP schedules above. PR G1-G3 runs are author evidence.

9. **PASS — disabled source and recorded section identity.**
   New production fields, publications, counter/drop/checks and call sites are cfg browser-runtime;
   futex itself is module-gated (`sched/mod.rs:17`). The disabled `timer_ring3_arg!` emits an empty
   string and the handler's extra argument is cfg'd out (`timer.rs:67`, `:352`). Test holds/syscalls
   use the dedicated scenario feature. [PR #161](https://github.com/SecondStageTurbine/LogansOS/pull/161)
   records NEO_BUILD_ID=kill-switch-fixed, default/desktop/usb-host .text/.rodata identity and 22
   differing .data bytes solely from panic-location line numbers. Those are inspected author
   artifacts, not independently rebuilt binaries or a claim of full binary identity by this reviewer.

10. **FAIL — CP-86 is marked guarded too broadly.**
    `docs/concurrency-pitfalls.md:132`, `:3894` onward claim refusal always reaches syscall return
    and a CPL0 IPI is covered by that return. Findings 1/2 falsify those statements. Add permanent
    late-join and IF=1 final-return-window discriminators and keep the item open until they pass.
    The AP no-tick fairness item is recorded as open (`:3926` onward), although its wording
    "never preempted, by anything" should distinguish the new retirement IPI from periodic scheduling.
    CP-15/21, CP-30/37 and the no-GPL-copy principle note are explicitly discussed in the PR;
    Linux zap_other_threads/signal_wake_up is attributed as GPL principle only. The added source
    is the project's MPL implementation; no vendored GPL code was found in this diff.
