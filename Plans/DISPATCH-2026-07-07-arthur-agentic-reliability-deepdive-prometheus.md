## The stakes (frame the research)
Arthur's whole premise (Article 1.5 conversation-first; OT-26 "safe to be wrong") rests on: NL → **valid** structured `Action` (the `action.rs` vocabulary) → kernel cap-check → NL result. The kernel cap-check makes a *wrong* action safe (denied/confirmed/undoable), but a model that emits *malformed* or *no* action just fails the interaction — the usability collapses. The lever is **structural reliability of the NL→Action step on a weak model.** This is heavily-researched externally (structured-output/constrained-decoding + small-model agents) — mine it, don't design from scratch.

## What to determine (real source — papers, the actual libraries' source/docs, model cards; byte-oracle-acquisition rule for any exact claim — read the real thing, never a summarizing fetch)
- **Q1 — Constrained/grammar-guided decoding: the SOTA + the mechanism.** How the proven techniques force a model to emit ONLY grammar-valid tokens: **GBNF grammars (llama.cpp), Outlines (regex/FSM→token-mask), XGrammar, guidance, JSON-schema-constrained decoding.** The *mechanism* that matters for us: they compute a per-step **token-validity mask** and apply it before sampling. Get concrete on: the compute cost of the mask (per-token), how the mask is built from a grammar, and the correctness guarantee (structural-valid vs semantic-valid — the model can still pick the wrong *valid* action). **This directly answers OT-26's banked A1 decision: logits-out-to-userland vs token-validity-bitmask-in-kernel** — which integration does the prior art favor on a measured data-movement basis, and what does it imply for our Article 6.6 (no policy in ring 0) line?
- **Q2 — Small-model agentic reliability: how weak models are made to succeed at tool-use.** Prior art on sub-3B (and 3B–8B) agentic reliability: the ReAct / function-calling formats, few-shot structure, the retry/self-correction loop, the **clarify-don't-guess fallback** (OT-26 D4 — grammar-*enabled* not *guaranteed*). What is the realistic tool-call success-rate ceiling for a 0.5B / 1.5B / 3B, and what raises it most (grammar constraint? format choice? few-shot? a tool-use-tuned model?). Cite measured numbers where they exist.
- **Q3 — Action-format design for small-model success.** Does the *format* Arthur emits change the success rate — verbose JSON vs a compact typed DSL (our `action.rs` is already a typed vocabulary)? What does prior art say maximizes weak-model success (token economy, unambiguous grammar, minimal nesting)? Recommend the concrete shape of Arthur's action grammar to maximize first-try validity.
- **Q4 — The model itself (feeds OT-38 next-brain).** Which small models are *best at agentic tool-use* today, not just general-capable: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct's native function-calling, tool-use-specialized small models (Hermes/functionary-style, the SmolLM/agent-tuned variants), and how they compare on tool-call benchmarks (BFCL/Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard, ToolBench). Does a tool-use-*specialized* small model beat a bigger general one for Arthur's needs? A note for the OT-38 brain choice.
- **Q5 — Failure-mode taxonomy × our safety boundary.** How small-model agents fail (hallucinated tool, malformed args, wrong-but-valid tool, refusal/loop) and — for each — how our **untrusted-NL / trusted-cap-check** model (Article 29.3) + OT-19 reversibility + the clarify fallback already contains it or doesn't. Cross-reference OT-26 §5.1's failure-mode table; flag any failure our current design does NOT yet cover.
