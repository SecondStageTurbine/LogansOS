## The one idea (frame everything around it)
**Never mutate the running system.** Build the next system state as a complete, verifiable artifact *beside* the current one; flip a pointer at boot; keep the old state bootable, bit-for-bit. Two independent VERIFIED implementations converge on exactly this move — Fedora Silverblue (rpm-ostree / libostree) and openSUSE MicroOS (transactional-update). One mechanism serves both audiences: the **beginner** gets unbreakability as a *default* (read-only base; every change power-loss-safe + reversible from the boot menu, no user care required); the **power user** gets depth through the *same* recorded-delta ledger (layering = reversible deltas on the pristine image; `reset`/`rollback` returns to known state). **LogansOS framing: this is our kill-switch discipline promoted from per-feature to whole-system** — our invariant "disabled ≡ the previous layer, bit-for-bit" becomes "the previous *deployment* is kept and bootable, bit-for-bit."
