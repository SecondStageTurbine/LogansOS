## 2. The design to resolve (D-block, ratifiable)
- **D1 — AEAD choice + implementation source (THE load-bearing decision — flag for PL ratification).** OT-17 ratified **AES-256-GCM**. But realize the tradeoff honestly: AES-256-GCM needs an **AES block-cipher + GHASH implementation we don't have** (from-scratch crypto is a footgun; a vetted no_std crate may or may not fit our ABI/build), whereas **ChaCha20-Poly1305 (RFC 8439) reuses 10.1's already-built, already-KAT'd ChaCha20** and adds only Poly1305 — a smaller, lower-risk surface for a from-scratch kernel, and a modern AEAD of equal standing. **State both, recommend one, and surface it as a genuine PL fork** (re-ratify D4's cipher, or hold AES-256-GCM). Whichever: **do not hand-roll the primitive without a raw-sourced KAT** (D5). Note AES-NI availability is irrelevant until there's an AES impl; ChaCha20 is constant-time in software already.
- **D2 — The encrypted record format + an independent byte oracle.** Define the on-disk layout of an encrypted `.mem` (versioned: `version || nonce || ciphertext || auth_tag`, per-record or per-file — decide and justify). **Format rule (CLAUDE.md):** ship a **hand-laid byte example** of one encrypted record (with a fixed key+nonce+plaintext → the exact ciphertext+tag bytes) asserted independently, NOT a writer↔reader round-trip. The nonce strategy is load-bearing: **GCM/Poly1305 nonce reuse under the same key is catastrophic** — specify how uniqueness is guaranteed (counter? random-per-record from `SYS_GETRANDOM`? — and the collision argument).
- **D3 — Key storage + lifecycle.** Per-category key at `.arthur/memory/<category>.key` (or justify another layout), generated from `SYS_GETRANDOM` at first write of that category. Read-protected by OT-17 D3's per-category cap (and, post-I-15, a narrow file cap). Specify generation, where it lives, and that the `.key` is **never** itself backed up by OT-19's undo (D4 below).
- **D4 — Crypto-erase = the forget mechanism (OT-17 D5 × OT-19 D2).** Forgetting a category = **burning its `.key`** (overwrite + unlink) ⇒ the `.mem` ciphertext (and any OT-19 undo backup, which is ciphertext under that same key) becomes permanently unrecoverable. Specify the burn (overwrite-then-unlink semantics on NeoFS) and prove the interaction: **undo backs up ciphertext under the per-category key, never the key**, so a key-burn crypto-erases the backup too (no forget-then-undo resurrection).
- **D5 — KAT sourcing (the oracle's acquisition).** The AEAD KAT vectors (NIST GCM KATs for AES-256-GCM, or RFC 8439 §2.8.2 for ChaCha20-Poly1305) MUST be pulled from the **raw authoritative source** (the NIST CAVP `.rsp` files or the RFC text / an upstream unit test like a crypto crate's KAT), **not via a summarizing WebFetch** (the byte-oracle corollary — a mangled KAT poisons the oracle). State which vectors, from where, and the exact expected bytes.
- **D6 — Fail-safe (OT-17 D6 extended to crypto).** Every failure degrades to the **ephemeral OD-6 window, never crash, never leak plaintext**: missing/corrupt `.key`, GCM/Poly1305 **auth-tag failure** (tamper/corruption), or a `SYS_GETRANDOM` failure ⇒ the category loads as ephemeral (or refuses to load), Arthur keeps running, no plaintext or key material leaks to a log/error. Auth-fail must be **fail-closed** (reject the record, do not surface decrypted-but-unverified bytes).
- **D7 — Kill-switch.** `arthur-memory-encryption` (sub-feature of `arthur-memory`) default-OFF ⇒ M1 plaintext behavior, bit-for-bit ≡ M1. On ⇒ encrypted-at-rest. The build's regression proves disabled ≡ M1.
