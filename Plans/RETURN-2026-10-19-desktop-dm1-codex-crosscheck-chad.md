# RETURN: DM-1 desktop motion cross-check (Chad)

**Reviewed:** merged DM-1 `9ee63db7` (#61), with pre-DM behavior at `35930c52`.

## Verdict

No findings survived re-verification. Items 1-7 **HOLD**.

## What I checked

1. **End frame — HOLDS.** Compared every `Scene::transition` call with the pre-DM present it replaced: launch open and focus, Arthur destroy focus/client erase, Home hold/resume/return, Lens view, pointer/keyboard focus changes, chip selection, bloom, and unbloom. Every `finals` list is the old present rectangle or the same two old Lens/selection rectangles. Every animated rect is contained by a final present; the larger old bounding presents intentionally cover more than the animated set. The WM is single-threaded through a transition, and the client/Lens planes used by composition are WM snapshots, so no current hook mutates the base or copied client between `T` and the end present. `end_mismatch` is narrow but not a tautology: it would catch a future focus/geometry/sprite/client-copy mutation during the paced frames, or any other state drift that makes the final `compose` differ from the saved `T`. It does not prove damage coverage; `uncovered` is the separate check.
2. **Damage — HOLDS.** `animate` intersects every rect with the screen before snapshotting or framing. `motion::frame` iterates exactly the supplied rectangle. Iris subtraction saturates at zero; the in-tree anchors and bounds are screen geometry, so the upper edges remain bounded by the clipped union. Each intermediate commit presents exactly one clipped rect. `extra_damage` is removed before `play` and unioned back after its `Result`, including error returns; an early `None` occurs before the take. Thus a pending sprite erase reaches the final present. `F` and `T` are copied for every rect that can be committed, and no intermediate present names an unsnapshotted rect.
3. **Kill-switch — HOLDS.** With `desktop-motion` absent, `transition` executes only `finals`. `desktop-home`-off Arthur exit remains on the old direct-present block. `move_selection` and `repaint_lens` now gather repaints before their old presents, but the regions are disjoint and the resulting pixels are unchanged; on the flat S1 renderer each repaint already redraws the same full final base. The ordinary Home, Lens, pointer, and resize feature sets do not imply motion. `desktop-full` intentionally does.
4. **Kernel gating — HOLDS.** Checked every constant, clock-id, dispatch-arm, and implementation cfg for `SYS_CLOCK_GETTIME` and `SYS_SCHED_YIELD`; all four sites add only `desktop-session`. A kernel without `desktop-session`, `browser-runtime`, and `atrium-pointer-forward` remains unchanged. In a desktop kernel the calls are ambient to all processes, but the clock is only a relaxed 100 Hz tick read and yield enters the existing scheduler path; neither conveys object authority or blocks another process.
5. **Input and ordering — HOLDS.** Launch/resume swap the route and complete the discard acknowledgement before animation; keys typed during the transition queue for Arthur. Hold/return swap to Home and discard before animation; later keys queue for Home. Pointer records remain in the existing bounded/coalescing route while the synchronous WM transition runs; button transitions retain the queue's existing protection. The boot-test hold consumes one decodable key only under its dedicated feature and on the WM-owned focus. It is armed inside the first actual hold, so Arthur exiting before any hold leaves no armed later transition; the post-transition cleanup also clears an arm if frame 3 never occurs.
6. **Oracle — HOLDS.** The Python oracle independently uses per-byte NumPy arithmetic and masks rather than Rust's two-lane blend. Its iris is close to a transliteration, as the THINK states. Frame 3 has different weights for full (`168/256`, N=10) and reduced (`224/256`, N=6), so the two mid-hold digests discriminate both profile and frame index. `HOLD_MASK_BOXES` removes the unknown Arthur pixels plus dynamic labels/text; it leaves the surrounding window/Home chrome and focus regions hashed, so it does not mask the transition itself. The synthetic destination fill also detects writes outside its two rects.
7. **Coverage — HOLDS, with stated gaps.** Confirmed the listed gaps: no upper wall-clock bound, no Reduce-Transparency-plus-motion QEMU run, no real-silicon timing, and no input-during-transition scenario. Additional explicit gaps are already accurately named in the THINK: no motion-off `desktop-full` QEMU combination, no preference-file QEMU leg, no call-site anchor oracle, and no QEMU mid-frame capture for Grow/Shrink or non-hold transitions. Also not directly exercised is recovery after an intermediate present error; the code preserves sprite erase damage, but normal callers treat the error as fatal.
