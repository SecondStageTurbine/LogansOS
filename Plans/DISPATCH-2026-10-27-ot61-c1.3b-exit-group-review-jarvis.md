**What it does.** When a process retires (exit_group or a fault kill), siblings are stopped wherever they are:
- **D1.** `Process.exiting`/`fault_killing` is stored before the drain.
- **D2.** `retire_current_if_dying()` runs at syscall entry, at syscall return, and in the reschedule-IPI and timer
  bodies when the interrupt came from CPL 3.
- **D3.** Each CPU publishes the pid it switches in, under its scheduler lock. After the drain, the reschedule IPI goes
  to every other CPU running that pid.
- **D4.** `futex::wait_on_key` refuses with `ESRCH` while the process is retiring.
- **The `RETIRING` counter** keeps the fast path at one load.

## Checklist

Answer each item PASS or FAIL with `file:line`. For any FAIL, give a concrete interleaving (CPU, instruction point,
lock state).

1. **The ordering argument.**
   - The flag store happens before the drain's lock-taking steps.
   - Every CPU publishes its pid under its scheduler lock before switching.
   - The drain's last pass takes every CPU's scheduler lock.
   So a sibling Running in ring 3 on another CPU either was drained or is seen by the IPI scan. Give the interleaving
   that would break it, or say why none exists.
2. **The retire points are safe.**
   - `retire_current_if_dying()` runs only where the thread holds no kernel lock or Arc: syscall entry before
     dispatch, syscall return, and an interrupt from CPL 3.
   - The timer stub's `mov rdi, [rbp+136]` reads the interrupted CS correctly for this frame layout.
   - Nothing retires a thread mid-syscall or from CPL 0.
3. **IRQ context (CP-37).** In the IPI and timer bodies, the PROCESSES and scheduler locks are taken only when the
   interrupt came from ring 3, so the CPU holds neither. Check that no IRQ-safe lock is held at those points and that
   EOI ordering is unchanged.
4. **The futex refusal (D4).**
   - The flag is read under `FUTEX_QUEUE`, which `purge_pid` also takes.
   - The `Arc<Process>` is fetched before the lock and dropped before parking (CP-30).
   - A refused waiter retires at syscall return.
   - std's futex sync handles `ESRCH` without looping forever.
5. **IPI targeting.**
   - The scan reads published pids without missing a CPU that switched in that pid after the drain.
   - A stale pid (the CPU has since switched away) causes only a harmless IPI.
   - pids aren't reused (`NEXT_PID` only grows).
6. **`RETIRING`.**
   - It increments before any retire point can see the flag and decrements in `Process::drop`.
   - A leaked `Arc<Process>` costs only the slow path, never a wrong retire of another process's thread.
7. **The fault kill.** It shares the tail and gets the same guarantees. Its twin scenario (a CPU-2 spinner during a
   fault → 139) would fail if the IPI were missing.
8. **The falsifications.** G1 (no IPI) hangs role 1. G2 (no futex refusal) hangs role 3. G3 (no syscall-return
   check) hangs role 4. Check that each would bite. Say whether the stated timer-path limit is accurate.
9. **Kill-switch.** With `browser-runtime` off and `NEO_BUILD_ID` pinned, `.text` and `.rodata` are identical and
   `.data` differs only in panic line numbers. Confirm the new code is cfg'd out.
10. **The pitfall bank and prior art.** CP-86 is now guarded, with its discriminator. The AP no-tick item is recorded
    as open. The Linux exit_group note is principle only, with no GPL code.
