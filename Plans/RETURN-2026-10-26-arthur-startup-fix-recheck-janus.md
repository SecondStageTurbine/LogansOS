Relaxed ordering does not tear the atomic word: competing PIDs 8 and 16 can leave either complete word, never one PID paired with the other's TSC. Eight slots mean collisions can evict even below nine committing PIDs; more than eight necessarily collide. The last store in that slot's modification order wins (not necessarily the latest sampled TSC if a writer is delayed). An evicted PID gets `None`; generation resets `THINK_TSC` first (`:150,158-159`), so the overlay renders `think=-` (`:329-340,389`).

   TSC is rounded down to a multiple of 1,048,576 cycles. The timestamp error is 0–1,048,575 cycles; the displayed negative think interval's magnitude can consequently be overestimated by less than `2^20 / tsc_hz` seconds before integer-ms formatting (`:316-322,339`). This remains the correct caller's interval at that resolution. Precision note: the comments' “under 1 ms at 1 GHz” is slightly inaccurate: the quantum is **1.048576 ms at 1 GHz**, or **0.524288 ms at 2 GHz**. A truncated timestamp of zero retains the existing unknown-marker behavior; this does not manufacture another PID's time.

2. **False match and #130 integration — PASS within the practical startup workload.** `kernel/src/ai/commit_slot_logic.rs:20` compares only low 20 bits; slot selection is modulo 8 (`kernel/src/ai/prefill_perf.rs:185,291`). Thus ordinary startup cannot encounter an alias; it requires over a million allocations in one boot and a retained matching slot or overlapping alias processes. This is a practical bound, not an absolute lifetime uniqueness guarantee: e.g. PIDs 8 and 1,048,584 do alias, and eventual u32 wrap is also outside that assumption.

   #130's formatting survives: `kernel/src/ai/prefill_perf.rs:346-363` still supplies `swiglu_cycles` and `attn_cycles` to `prefill_perf_logic::write_perf_line`; that pure formatter is unchanged by #131. No old split PID/TSC slot read remains.
