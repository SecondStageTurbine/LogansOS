## The two seams to REFUTE (cite file:line + the concrete cross-CPU interleaving)
1. **Per-CPU `live_count` consistency vs. the last-reap `Pmap` drop.** The per-process teardown drops `Arc<Process>`/`Arc<Pmap>` **only when `live_count == 0`** (last thread reaped). But `live_count` is accounted **per-CPU** (admit/reap on the owning CPU), while `zombify_thread` pushes the zombie to `current_scheduler()` (`scheduler.rs`). **The refute target:** can a same-process thread be admitted/counted on CPU A while another exits/zombifies/reaps on CPU B, such that one CPU's `live_count` reaches 0 **while a sibling thread is still live on another CPU** → the `Process`/`Pmap` Arc is dropped early → **use-after-free of the shared address space**? Or the mirror: a count that never reaches 0 → `Pmap` leak. Trace whether C1.1's scheduler EVER runs two threads of one process on different CPUs (does `try_push_ready`/admission ever place a sibling on a peer CPU, or are a process's threads pinned to the creator's CPU?). If they can split across CPUs, is the `live_count`/last-reap decision race-free? The AAG could not tell from the diff.
2. **Force-kill vs. join wake/zombify race on the done-word.** `thread_join` parks on the target's done-word (futex) after dropping its owned `Arc<Process>` (raw pointers held). A concurrent **force-kill** (`fault_kill_current_user` / `purge_pid`) zombifies the parked joiner and/or the target, relying on **generation-checked tokens** (`Stale`/`Gone`) rather than lock exclusion to prevent a stale wake/dereference. **Refute target:** construct a cross-CPU interleaving where force-kill on CPU A and a join-wake/exit on CPU B race such that (a) the joiner dereferences a freed registry/done-word, or (b) a wake is lost/mis-delivered, or (c) a token generation check is defeated (ABA on the token index). Is the generation check sufficient across CPUs, or does it need the scheduler lock held across the check+act?
