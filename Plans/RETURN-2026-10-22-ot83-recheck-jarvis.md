2. **CLOSED — no production admission trace flushes under `QUEUES`; STILL OPEN — literal all-locks `try_lock` requirement.**

   Enforcing lines: all three trace helpers call `trace_line` (`kernel/src/ai/admission.rs:423`, `:432`, `:439`), which masks interrupts and enters/leaves stage-only at `:409–413`. This includes the deferred trace before parking (`:235`), admission and dead-token withdrawal in `complete` (`:320`, `:338`), and cancellation withdrawal (`:375`).    Every admission `flush_pending` site follows release of `QUEUES`: `submit` at `:145/:147` and `:154/:156`; immediate admission at `:226/:228`; deferred resume at `:241–248` after the park primitive has released the guard on the new stack; successful completion at `:334/:335`. `cancel_pid` drops the guard at `:381` without flushing. Early returns and exhausted/dead-token completion paths can leave staged output for a later drain; none adds a flush under the guard.

   `block_sink.rs:674` tries `SINK` once and returns on contention, preserving `FLUSH_PENDING`. However, `:679` calls `sink.inner.lock()`, not `try_lock()`. `inner` is private (`:61`); `append` is private (`:189`) and called by the private writer under `SINK`; the panic, staged, and flush entry points also retain `SINK` while accessing it. Consequently a suspended inner-lock holder also retains `SINK`, making `:674` fail before `:679` is reached. No inner-lock contention witness survives in this implementation. To satisfy the requested literal contract and CP-71's “try-lock only” wording, use an inner `try_lock` too, retaining the pending flag if it fails. This is not a P1 bounce of the crash-containment fix.

5. **CLOSED for new containment hazards — nesting, reaper drain, and IF assumptions checked, with the qualifications above.**

   Enforcing lines: `block_sink.rs:626` atomically sets the CPU bit and returns its prior state; `:632–633` clears it only for the outermost section. Nested admission traces therefore leave the enclosing kill section active. The 64-bit mask matches `per_cpu::MAX_CPUS = 64` (`kernel/src/arch/x86_64/per_cpu.rs:15`), with initialized CPU IDs bounded before use. These APIs rely on IF=0 and valid kernel GS; they do not enforce those preconditions themselves. Production exception entry establishes kernel GS (`idt.rs:566`, `:754`) and runs with interrupts masked; admission independently uses `without_interrupts` (`admission.rs:409`), which restores the caller's prior IF. No reviewed stage-only section yields or switches while active. Clearing before exit avoids carrying a CPU bit into the next thread.

   The reaper calls `flush_pending` at `scheduler.rs:2623`, after popping under the scheduler guard and releasing it (`:2573–2588`), freeing the thread, and finishing process reap (`:2611–2620`). A busy `SINK` causes an immediate return, so the suspended holder can run again. The pending flag is cleared only after acquiring `SINK` (`block_sink.rs:677`); staged writers also hold `SINK` while setting it (`:664`), preventing a concurrent stage from losing its pending indication. Drain is best effort and needs a later caller if a try fails. It can perform synchronous block I/O after acquiring the locks; “never waits” must be read as the sink-lock acquisition claim, subject to item 2, not a promise that device completion never waits. Admission's post-trace drains can inherit IF=0 from syscalls; this fix does not generally make all logging or all flush callers I/O-free. None of those drains is called by the reviewed exception-kill path.
