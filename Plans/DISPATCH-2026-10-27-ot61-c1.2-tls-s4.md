## Scope

1. **The opening spike (ratified amendment P3), reported before you add any TLS syscall.** Build the key-only
   gate: an A4-shape scanner that fails the browser-runtime build on any final ELF carrying `PT_TLS` or `SHF_TLS`
   (a `.tdata`/`.tbss` section, a native-TLS relocation or a `__thread` symbol). Prove that the `std` consumer
   compiles and links clean under it, with Rust `has-thread-local=false` and `singlethread=false`, and with C0.5's
   forced `no_threads` route replaced by a Neoplutarch OS-key/PAL route.
   For the SpiderMonkey side, say exactly what you can and can't check in this environment (for example, Mozilla's
   `HAVE_THREAD_TLS_KEYWORD` forced off, from the 2026-07-22 servo-host spike's notes), and don't claim more.
   If a consumer genuinely can't build key-only, stop and report. The reserved `PT_TLS` rung is a decision for J.c.
   and the PM.
2. **If the spike is clean, C1.2 itself:**
   - per-thread FS.base on `Thread`, saved and restored in the context switch (`WRFSBASE` or the MSR), for the
     initial thread and every child thread;
   - `thread_set_tls(base)` in the reserved C1 syscall block, gated on the C1 capability like the other C1 syscalls;
   - the userland key layer in `neo-std-pal`: `create(dtor)`, `set`, `get` and `delete`, with destructors at thread
     exit using the amended rule (clear before calling, up to 4 passes, generation check). They run before the exit
     syscall;
   - the TCB/key-block/errno slot installed by C1.1's TLS-free bootstrap trampoline.
3. **Kill-switch.** `browser-runtime` off gives `.text`/`.rodata` byte-identical to today, as C1.1 does. The new code
   is reachable only under the C1 capability.

## Battery (QEMU, in the gate)

- Two threads see distinct per-key values (isolation).
- FS.base survives a context switch and a cross-CPU migration.
- A destructor that re-sets its key is re-run and converges within 4 passes, before the exit syscall.
- A thread without the C1 capability gets `EPERM`.
- The key-only gate fires on a deliberate `__thread`/`.tdata` leak-in (falsification).
