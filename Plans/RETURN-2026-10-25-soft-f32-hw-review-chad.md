## Why the finite arithmetic matches

For add, Finite decodes an exact integer significand and power of two. When exponent separation is at most 31, signed shifted significands and their sum fit i64 (24+31 bits plus carry), and pack rounds once to nearest-even. For separation above 31, the smaller operand is far below half an ulp of the larger normal operand, so returning the larger operand is also the nearest-even result. Opposite-sign cancellation is computed exactly before packing. For multiply, the exact product of two at-most-24-bit significands fits u64; pack performs the same single nearest-even rounding, gradual underflow and infinity overflow as SSE with FTZ/DAZ off. `pack` at soft_f32.rs:196 and `round_shift_right` at :244 implement these paths. The shift==64 branch's tie behavior is not reached with a 2^63 mantissa from add/mul: their mantissas have at most 56/48 bits.

The edge set includes max finite and neighbors, and every exponent with mantissas 0, 1, 0x400000, 0x7fffff and 0x555555 of both signs. For example MAX+2^102 rounds to MAX, while MAX+2^103 overflows; both pairs are present. All four zero combinations are present. This is strong structured and random evidence plus source reasoning, not an enumeration of all 2^64 operand pairs or every NaN payload.

Direct soft_f32 users are ops/mod.rs, ops/gdn.rs, forward_logic.rs and generate_logic.rs (the latter reaches the new operations through exp at :603). The graph path goes through synchronous `ai/exec.rs:47`, execution dispatch and CPU driver (`drivers/accel/accel_cpu.rs:126`); the simulator's CPU reference executes in poll/wait (`drivers/accel/accel_sim.rs:350`, `:619`, `:672`), not its ISR. Qwen inventory calls GDN and attention forward at `ai/qwen_inventory.rs:603` and :646. Generate/infer syscalls are selected below the normalized entry (`arch/x86_64/syscall.rs:1012`-`:1041`). Tests and boot-side work occur after enable_sse. I found no caller of these soft arithmetic routines in an ISR, before FPU setup, or directly on user MXCSR. Virtio-accel's ISR retires device completions; the software reference is in the synchronous CPU/simulator paths.

The legacy no-xsave-fpu switch does not itself save XMM: cooperative calls obey the ABI and asynchronous timer/IRQ stubs save the interrupted SSE image, as documented at context.rs:16. The product's xsave-fpu additionally preserves full per-thread SSE/AVX state. This matches the existing hardware matmul boundary rule.

MXCSR equality means control bits, not sticky status bits: the debug check masks with 0xffc0 (`soft_f32.rs:94`), allowing arithmetic flags. **Image A is opt-level 3 in the dev profile, not the release profile.** `tools/xtask/src/main.rs:4846` explicitly keeps debug assertions on; `:6035` sets only CARGO_PROFILE_DEV_OPT_LEVEL, and Cargo.toml:88 does not disable them. Thus the product retains this per-operation assertion under the repository's normal build settings. Independently, entry/boot normalization and saved/restored FP state establish the environment; #128 adds no unconditional release-mode repair, fallback, or guard if a future build disables debug assertions.

`with_integer_ops` is not panic-safe (its reset is after f returns), but the reviewed comparisons do not catch panics and continue testing on that same thread; an oracle failure fails the test. This does not make the successful paired comparisons vacuous.
