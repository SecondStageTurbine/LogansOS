**Scope.** No installer writes yet (that's R4.3). R4.1 is the claim machinery and its tests:
- a registry generation, with device tokens `(slot, generation)`;
- driver-reported identity (NVMe Identify, ATA IDENTIFY, virtio `GET_ID`), where an empty serial means "identity
  unavailable";
- a per-device `media_generation` that the drivers bump on removal, reset or a fatal state;
- the claim, holding `Arc<GuardedDevice>`, the identity snapshot and the expected fingerprint;
- `ClaimedWriter`, which checks the claim and `media_generation` before submission and after completion.

## Checklist

Answer each item PASS or FAIL, with the `file:line` that enforces it. For a FAIL, give a concrete sequence.

1. **The claim is the only path past the guard, and it's narrow.**
   - Nothing but `ClaimedWriter` writes past the guard.
   - A claim names one device object, is one-shot, and can't be cloned, re-armed, or moved to another device.
   - With `installer` off, none of this is compiled and the guard is today's.
2. **Tokens can't be forged or reused.**
   - A token from before a re-registration, a removal or a dead-device report is refused.
   - A token can't be built from userland; say how tokens cross the syscall boundary, if they do in R4.1.
3. **The pre/post `media_generation` checks.**
   - A mismatch before submission means not submitted.
   - A mismatch after completion stops everything: no further write.
   - Check the reads are ordered correctly around the submission (no reordering past the doorbell or notify).
4. **Driver reporting.** For each of NVMe, AHCI and virtio-blk, the events §2a item 5 lists bump `media_generation`
   (removal, reset, namespace or config change, fatal status). Name any event that doesn't, and whether the THINK
   relies on it.
5. **"Identity unavailable" fails closed.** An empty, blank or non-printable serial makes the disk ineligible for a
   destructive claim. A same-serial twin isn't treated as the same disk (the fingerprint differs).
6. **The stated limit is honest.** The masked-swap KAT (relay #88's case) asserts the limit, not a stronger bound, and
   the PR states the limit plainly.
7. **Memory.** Only the live claim holds the 2 MiB of expected bytes, and enumeration keeps only the digests (S3's
   noted deviation from §2a item 3). Check no path keeps per-row bytes, and that a failed allocation fails closed.
8. **Test-only items stay out of the product.** `neo-test-install-claim` and any swap hooks compile only under
   `neo-test-*` features and are absent from `PRODUCT_IMAGE_KERNEL_FEATURES`.
9. **Coverage.** Name what the gate doesn't cover. For example: real NVMe/AHCI media-change events (QEMU can't hotplug
   these in the gate), and a SATA disk behind a port multiplier.
